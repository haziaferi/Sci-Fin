Budget — grow, then split, then centre

A third rule, neither A nor B from the previous sheet: the column grows with the window while there is only one of it; when the content area is wide enough for two, it splits; after that it stops growing and the pair stays centred. And because splitting creates vertical room in the left column, the collapsed “6 recurring expenses” summary unwraps into its six items rather than leaving that room empty.

Drawn with the seed's real recurring expenses (prototype-seed.json): Rent 950, Car loan 260, Car insurance 600 yearly, Phone bill 45, Pest control 90 bimonthly, Streaming 14 — a monthly equivalent of €1,364. The earlier sheets carried €1,095/mo, which does not match this fixture and is corrected here.

The rule in three phases, with content = client − 89:

1content < 1076 — one column, filling the whole content area. It grows as the window grows. Recurring expenses stays collapsed to one row.

2content ≥ 1076 — two columns of 530 with a 16 gap. Recurring expenses unwraps to six rows.

3beyond that — the 1076 block stays 1076 and centres. Extra width is left empty, exactly as the settled measure-cap rule does for Learn and Settings.

Two consequences worth deciding on, both visible above.

1 · The app's current window is on the wrong side of the threshold. At client 1084 the content is 995, under 1076, so this rule gives one column at the size the app opens at today. The split would only appear once the window is dragged past client 1165. If the split should be the default, either the threshold drops — split as soon as two columns of the 368 floor fit, at content 752, with the columns then growing from 368 up to 530 before centring — or the app's default window grows.

2 · The unwrap changes what the screen is. Collapsed, the row says 6 recurring expenses · €1,364/mo; unwrapped it lists Rent, Car loan, Car insurance, Phone bill, Pest control and Streaming with their cadences. That is a real gain in a wide window — but it means the same destination shows different information at different window sizes, which no rule so far has done. Worth being deliberate about rather than getting for free.
