Withdrawn in framing, 2026-09-24. These frames are drawn at one window size, as though the app had a fixed 1100 × 760. It does not — the window is resizable, and §2.5 already says every measurement is continuous in window width. Read every number here as one sample of a rule, not as a constant: 506 | 505 is what “divide the content in two” gives at one width; 530 centred is what “hold a measure and centre it” gives at one width. The rules, measured from four shipping apps under a two-axis resize sweep, are in design/desktop-prior-art-2026-09-23.md §15. The geometry below was measured correctly and the comparisons between options still hold — every option was sampled at the same size. What does not hold is treating any single figure as the answer.

The desktop — the five destinations

Every earlier sheet drew alternatives: six ways the desktop could be arranged. This one draws the app that was decided. §4.2 fixes five destinations — Budget, Activity, Learn, Reports, Settings — and those are what is in scope. Each frame is 1100 × 760, in Slate light, with the parity fixture's figures and the real strings from the build.

On the window: the titlebar drawn above each frame is Windows 11's own, 31px, measured with GetSystemMetrics — Main.kt:57 opens a decorated Window(), so the app never paints it. The 1100 × 760 below it is the client area. Note that rememberWindowState(1100, 760) sets AWT's outer bounds, so the running app's client area is smaller than these frames assume; the sidebar sheet has the detail. It does not affect any comparison here, only the absolute widths.

The rule these five are drawn under, taken from the 2026-09-23 walks. Three of the four surveyed apps hold a shell constant and vary exactly one thing; the fourth varies everything and is the one that reads as incoherent. So §4.8 has to name both halves:

The constant: an 88px rail of five destinations, and one content area of 1011px. Nothing else is permanent.

The one thing that varies: the measure. Tables and charts take the full content width; prose and settings take 530 centred. That is Actual's rule, measured — 849 for tables, 530 for Settings — and it is the part of the withdrawn option C that survives.

The one named exception: Budget splits into two columns, because it is the only destination in §4.2's table with two co-equal views of one thing. Naming it as an exception is what keeps it from becoming a precedent.

Three things these frames deliberately do not settle, because they are still yours:

1 · The rail. Drawn at 88px because that is what §4.1 fixes today — but zero of nine surveyed apps use an icon rail, and the two best-regarded use a data or text sidebar at 240 and 160. Every frame here would accept a 240px sidebar with the destinations named instead; nothing else in the layout would change.

2 · The scale ceiling. These are drawn at 1.0×. The measurements say the ceiling is a small decision — across 1.0× to 2.0× a 1920 window moves only from 47% to 66% filled, because text may not scale with the window.

3 · Whether the 530 measure is right for this app. It is Actual's number, measured, not a derivation from §2.5. If Sci-Fin's type ramp wants a different measure, it should be set from the ramp rather than borrowed.
