Withdrawn in framing, 2026-09-24. These frames are drawn at one window size, as though the app had a fixed 1100 × 760. It does not — the window is resizable, and §2.5 already says every measurement is continuous in window width. Read every number here as one sample of a rule, not as a constant: 506 | 505 is what “divide the content in two” gives at one width; 530 centred is what “hold a measure and centre it” gives at one width. The geometry below was measured correctly and the comparisons between options still hold — every option was sampled at the same size. What does not hold is treating any single figure as the answer.

The same five, with a 240px sidebar

The same six frames as the decided sheet, with one thing changed: the 88px icon rail becomes a 240px sidebar with the destinations named. Nothing else was edited. The six navigation blocks are byte-identical between the two files — same class, same five entries, same glyphs — and only the CSS rule behind them differs. The earlier sheet claimed “every frame here would accept a 240px sidebar; nothing else in the layout would change.” This file is that claim carried out, so it can be checked rather than believed.

The rule is unchanged — only the number in its first clause moves. Three of the four surveyed apps hold a shell constant and vary exactly one thing; the fourth varies everything and is the one that reads as incoherent. So §4.8 still has to name both halves:

The constant: a 240px sidebar naming five destinations, and one content area of 860px. That pair is not derived — it is exactly what Actual Budget measures at in a 1100px window, sidebar 240, content 860. Nothing else is permanent.

The one thing that varies: the measure. Tables and charts take the full content width; prose and settings take 530 centred. That is Actual's rule, measured — 849 for tables, 530 for Settings — and it is the part of the withdrawn option C that survives.

The one named exception: Budget splits into two columns, because it is the only destination in §4.2's table with two co-equal views of one thing. Naming it as an exception is what keeps it from becoming a precedent.

Fill does not decide this one

Taking 151px away ought to make every screen visibly denser. It does not — and since “fill the space” was the criterion that produced three wrong recommendations in this survey, it is worth measuring rather than assuming. Both files were scored with one identical script: leaf elements only, their area over the body's area, so the two columns can be compared to each other.

Nothing moves more than 1.6 points in either direction, and the three wide screens move the “wrong” way — slightly emptier, not denser. The reason is that almost every element on these screens is a stretch-width block: narrow the pane and the cards, bars and table rows narrow with it, so the proportion they cover barely changes. Only Learn and Settings behave as intuition predicts, and only because their 530 measure is the one thing that does not stretch.

So fill is not an argument here, in either direction. The decision rests on what the 151px is spent on — five words instead of five glyphs — and on Budget's two columns, which are the only place the width does real work.

These figures were re-measured after the titlebar correction below raised the client area from 726 to 760. Every number moved, and every delta stayed the same to within 0.2 — which is the useful part: the conclusion does not depend on getting the window height exactly right.

A note on the window itself

These frames wore macOS traffic lights until this revision — a habit, not a decision. Main.kt:57 opens a plain decorated Window(title = “Sci-Fin”), so the app wears the native Windows 11 titlebar: icon and title on the left, minimise, maximise and close on the right. Measured on this machine with GetSystemMetrics — SM_CYCAPTION 23 + SM_CYSIZEFRAME 4 + SM_CXPADDEDBORDER 4 — it is 31px, and it is now drawn that way.

Correcting it surfaced something worth more than the styling. rememberWindowState(width = 1100.dp, height = 760.dp) sets AWT's outer bounds, which include the decoration — but every sheet in this series has treated 1100 × 760 as the client area. So the app as it runs today has less room than all six frames assume: at least 31px less vertically, plus whatever the DWM resize frame takes at the sides, which GetSystemMetrics alone cannot settle.

Two things follow. No comparison in this series changes — every option was drawn under the same assumption, so rail-versus-sidebar, 506-versus-430 and the fill deltas above are all unaffected. But the absolute claims are provisional, including “content 860, exactly Actual's 860”: if the client area is really 1084 wide, a 240px sidebar leaves 844. Settling it needs one measurement of the running window — the client rect of the real Sci-Fin window, not a system metric — and until that is taken these frames describe a 1100 × 760 client area that the current WindowState does not actually deliver.

What does decide it: the OS text size

§2.5 puts the type ramp on the OS axis, not the window axis, and TextScale runs SM 0.875 → XL 1.5. A shell that names its destinations has to hold the longest of them at every one of those. The available width is a fixed budget: the glyph and the padding are on the screen axis and do not grow with the text, so all the growth has to come out of the label.

Available label width, read off Frame.kt and the sidebar rule in this file: the 88px rail gives 76dp (88 less u/2 twice for the column and u/4 twice for the item); a sidebar row gives its width less 81dp of border, padding, 20dp glyph and gap — so 79dp at 160 and 159dp at 240. The longest label is “Settings”.

Two figures per cell because the rail's label is xs (16 × factor × 0.846) and a sidebar row's is base (16 × factor). 160 is the only one of the three that breaks, and it breaks on “Settings” — a destination every user visits, and a single word, so wrapping is not available as a rescue. Compose would break it inside the word. That is why there is no 160 frame in this file.

Two honest limits on this table. The widths are canvas measurements in Segoe UI, which is what Compose Desktop resolves to on Windows but is not the same rasteriser — so the rail's 1dp of headroom at XL is inside my measurement error and should be confirmed by running the app at XL rather than trusted from here. And that 1dp is the rail's whole margin: step 8's queued “System” text-size default would let the OS drive the factor past 1.5 — Windows offers up to 2.25× — and above roughly 1.52× the 88px rail's labels start breaking within the word too. The 240px sidebar is the only one of the three with room for that change.

What this sheet settles and what it does not. It settles that the swap is cheap: two destinations lose no width at all, and the other three lose width without losing content. It does not settle these, which are still yours:

1 · Rail or sidebar — the decision this file exists to let you make, now that both are drawn. The survey evidence points one way: zero of nine surveyed apps use an icon rail, and the two best-regarded use a named sidebar at 240 and 160. The counter-evidence is Sci-Fin's own §4.1, which fixes 88 today, and the 151px above. Changing it is a §4.1 amendment, not just a redraw, so it needs your word before anything is coded.

1b · 160 was considered and is ruled out, on the measurement in the section above rather than on taste. Fava's sidebar is 160 and would have cost 71px instead of 151 — but it is the only one of the three shells whose labels stop fitting inside Sci-Fin's own text-scale range.

2 · The scale ceiling. These are drawn at 1.0×. The measurements say the ceiling is a small decision — across 1.0× to 2.0× a 1920 window moves only from 47% to 66% filled, because text may not scale with the window.

3 · Whether the 530 measure is right for this app. It is Actual's number, measured, not a derivation from §2.5. If Sci-Fin's type ramp wants a different measure, it should be set from the ramp rather than borrowed.
