The desktop as rules, not numbers

Every earlier sheet drew the app at one window size, as though 1100 × 760 were the size it has. It is not — the window is resizable to anything. This sheet draws the same five destinations as rules, each shown at three widths, so what you are judging is the behaviour rather than a snapshot of it.

The rules are not invented. They were measured on 2026-09-24 from four applications installed on this machine — Notion, Obsidian, VS Code and Todoist — each driven through a two-axis resize sweep, down and back up through the same widths and heights. Full method and figures: design/desktop-prior-art-2026-09-23.md §15.

What the four apps agree on, unanimously.

1 · Navigation chrome is a fixed pixel size and never scales with the window. Not one of the four grows its rail or sidebar as the window grows. Notion holds 267px from a 644px window to a 1904px one — 41% of the window at the narrow end, 14% at the wide end. The same holds vertically: three of four have top-chrome rows that do not move by a single pixel across a 2× height range.

2 · All the variation lives in the content area, by exactly three mechanisms — reveal (Notion: keep a measure, let extra width be empty), divide (Obsidian: split the remainder exactly in half), and centre to a maximum measure (VS Code: fill below a threshold, hold and centre above it).

3 · Collapse is governed by content, not by window width. Todoist's sidebar is 280px, and it drops to 66px between client 744 and 764 — exactly where keeping it would push the content below ~484px. It is not collapsing at a width; it is collapsing to protect a measure.

4 · Resizing is exactly reversible. Every app returns to an identical layout at a given size whatever path it took there. No app remembers having been narrow.

What this sheet settles and what it does not.

Settled by measurement: the form of every rule above — chrome constant, content varying by one of three named mechanisms, collapse governed by a content floor rather than a window breakpoint, and resizing exactly reversible. Four apps out of four, both directions, on both axes.

Still yours — 1 · the rail. Drawn at 88 because §4.1 fixes it; zero of nine surveyed apps use an icon rail, and the measured alternatives are 240 (Actual, Todoist expanded 280) and 160 (Fava). §14.4 rules 160 out on text scaling. Nothing in this sheet changes if the answer is 240 — the rule is that it is constant.

Still yours — 2 · the two candidate numbers. The prose measure (drawn 530, Actual's) and the per-column floor (drawn 368, §4.8's own list pane). These are the only two constants the rules need, and both should be derived from §2.5's type ramp rather than borrowed from another app.

Still yours — 3 · §2.5's open desktop half. The phone half is settled (width ÷ 360, floored 0.85). The measurement says the desktop half is not a smaller version of the same question: nothing on the desktop should multiply chrome by a window factor, because no shipping app does. A navigation label does not become easier to read because the window got wider.
